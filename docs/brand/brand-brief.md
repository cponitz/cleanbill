# Clean Bill — Brand & Copy Brief
*Creative direction and page copy for the Claude Design assistant. Draft v1, Sep 16, 2026. Copied unchanged from the Cowork project (`claude/clean-bill-brand-brief.md`) for SPEC-08 Part C; save as `docs/brand/brand-brief.md`.*

Glossary: **TCAD** = Travis Central Appraisal District (the county body that approves homestead exemptions). **Form 50-114** = the Texas residence homestead exemption application. **Contingency fee** = a fee owed only if a result is produced. **§41.0051** = the Texas Property Code section that requires the advertisement disclosure at the bottom of every page.

## 2. What Clean Bill is

**The company in one sentence:** Clean Bill helps people and businesses run the tightest, cleanest expense structure possible — starting with the money they're already owed.

**Launch product:** Late homestead-exemption filings for Travis County homeowners. Texas law lets a homeowner claim up to two prior years of overpaid property tax if they never filed their exemption. Most homeowners who qualify don't know it. We identify them from public appraisal records, prepare Form 50-114, submit it as they direct, monitor TCAD until the refund is issued, and only then invoice 25% of what they actually received.

**The road map (say it on the page, lightly):** individual energy-credit filings; utility, insurance and telecom bill audits and overcharge recovery for businesses. The brand has to hold all of these, which is why the name is *Clean Bill* and not *Texas Refund* anything.

**The name, explained for the designer:** "A clean bill of health." Every service we will ever offer ends in a bill someone is overpaying — a tax bill, a utility bill, a phone bill, an insurance premium. Clean Bill is the checkup that either confirms you're paying what you should, or finds what you're not.

## 3. Who we're talking to, and how they see themselves

Our customer is not someone who needs rescuing. They are the person in the family or the business who *already* reads the fine print, keeps the receipts and tracks the budget. They didn't file this exemption because nobody told them it existed — not because they're careless. The worst thing our copy can do is imply they made a mistake.

So every line is written to confirm a flattering, true story: **"I'm the kind of person who doesn't leave money on the table — and I just found some."**

Three self-images to speak to, in order of priority:

1. **Prudent.** They make careful, unflashy decisions. We match that register: plain math, no hype, no countdown timers.
2. **In control.** They want to see how it works before they say yes. We show them the entire process, including how to do it themselves for free.
3. **Fair-minded.** They'll pay for real work, but they hate being taken. We price like a partner, and we take on the risk.

**Words that belong to this customer:** careful, sensible, prudent, tidy, in order, well run, squared away, tight ship, no waste, rightfully yours.

**Words that never appear:** unclaimed money!, act now, limited time, you may be eligible, government-sounding anything, "we'll fight for you," exclamation points.

## 4. The core promise and its three supports

**Promise:** *Pay what you should. Not a dollar more.*

Supports (these become the three columns of the page):

- **We find it.** We do the research before you ever hear from us. If we wrote to you, we already believe you're owed money, and we'll show you the number.
- **We handle it.** You answer five questions and sign. We prepare the paperwork, submit it, and follow it until the county pays.
- **You pay last.** The refund goes to you, not to us. We invoice after it arrives. If nothing arrives, you owe nothing.

## 6. Brand voice

**Sounds like:** a sharp friend who happens to be a CPA (Certified Public Accountant), explaining something over coffee. Calm, specific, a little dry. Confident enough to tell you how to do it without us.

**Rules:**

- Lead with the number, then the mechanism, then the ask. Never the ask first.
- Say "you're owed" not "you could be eligible." Say "the county" not "the government."
- Show the math in the open — the fee, the fee base, what's excluded — before anyone asks.
- Every trust claim is a *fact* (an address, a statute, a dollar figure), never an adjective ("trusted," "reliable," "expert").
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Read every line aloud; if it sounds like a mailer, cut it.
- Never invoke urgency we didn't cause. The one real deadline (the 2024 tax year falls off on Feb 1, 2027) is stated once, as a fact with a date, not as pressure.

## 7. Value first — what we give before we ask for anything

This is the heart of the psychology. The page should give the reader three things of real value *before* the first form field:

1. **Their number.** "Based on the appraisal district's records for your address, we estimate you're owed **$3,700**." Specific, sourced, and theirs. (On a generic homepage, use the median: *"The median refund on the homes we've identified is about $3,700."*)
2. **The do-it-yourself path, in full.** A short, sincere section: *"You can do this yourself for free."* Link to Form 50-114, list the three things TCAD needs, note the driver's-license address rule. We mean it. The reader who takes this path was never going to pay anyone; the reader who doesn't now trusts us completely.
3. **The rules of the game.** Who actually owes the refund (name the taxing units), how long it takes (TCAD up to 90 days, then the Tax Office up to 60), and the one deadline that matters. Knowledge is the gift; it also happens to be what §41.0051 requires us to disclose.

---

## 8. The trust inversion — we trust you, not the other way around

Most services in this category make the customer take all the risk: pay upfront, sign an agent designation, hand over control. We flip it and *say so out loud.* This deserves its own section with its own headline.

**Headline:** *We're the ones taking the risk here.*

**Body:**

> Here's how this actually works. We do the research, prepare your filing and follow it through the county — all before we've earned anything. When the refund is approved, the county sends the money to *you*. Not to us. It lands in your account, and then we send you an itemized invoice for 25% of what you received.
>
> That means for a few months, we've done all the work and you're holding all the money. We're comfortable with that. We built this company for careful people, and careful people pay for work that was done well.
>
> If the county issues no refund, there's no invoice. If they issue less than we estimated, the fee shrinks with it. There is no upfront charge, no agent designation, no contract that outlives the refund.

**Supporting facts to place nearby (as a plain list, not badges):**
- Filing with TCAD is free, and we tell you how.
- 25% of the refund you actually receive. $0 otherwise.
- No fee on your lower future bills — those are yours.
- You sign your own application. We never sign for you.
- We are not affiliated with TCAD, the Tax Office or any government agency.

*(Product note for Charlie, not for the page: the operating model keeps a card on file at signup and charges it after the refund is issued. The copy above is written to be true under that model — "no upfront charge," "we invoice after." If you want the purest version of the trust inversion, make the card step optional on the page, as the draft service agreement already allows.)*

### How it works (three steps — a real sequence, so numbering is honest)
1. **We show you the number.** We pulled your property's record from the appraisal district. If you're on this page, we think you're owed a refund, and we'll tell you our estimate before you enter anything.
2. **You answer five questions and sign.** Confirm it's your home, snap a photo of your Texas driver's license, sign your own application. Ten minutes on your phone.
3. **The county pays you. Then you pay us.** We submit, monitor and respond to TCAD until the refund is issued to you. After it arrives, we send an itemized invoice for 25%.

## 10. Creative direction for the designer

**Feeling:** A clean bill of health. Fresh, orderly, quiet confidence. Closer to a well-run credit union or a good accountant's office than to a fintech app. Nothing that looks like a government notice; nothing that looks like a coupon.

**Palette (proposal):**
- **Ground:** clean white `#FFFFFF` with a cool paper tint for panels `#F3F6F4`
- **Ink:** deep green-black `#12261F` for text (green = clean, health, money — without being "cash green")
- **Primary:** forest `#1E5B45` for headlines, buttons, the table header
- **Accent (use once per screen):** warm amber `#C9922E` — the "found money" highlight on the refund number and the *You keep* row
- **Rule/borders:** `#D7DED9`
- Avoid: red, urgency yellow, gradients, anything that reads as "alert."

**Type:** a humanist serif for headlines (Source Serif 4 or Fraunces at a low optical size — trustworthy, slightly literary) paired with a clean grotesk for body and UI (Inter Tight or IBM Plex Sans). Tabular figures everywhere a dollar sign appears.

**Signature visual:** a single motif — a bill or invoice with a clean check mark, rendered as a simple line drawing. Or a "receipt strip" showing the math in Section 9. Not a house, not a piggy bank, not a stock photo of a smiling couple.

**Layout notes:**
- The hero is the promise plus the number. No full-viewport hero; the reader should see "How it works" without scrolling on a laptop.
- "We're the ones taking the risk here" gets a full-width panel in the primary green with white text — it's the point-of-view section.
- The DIY section is *not* buried or greyed. Give it the same weight as the CTA. That's the whole trick.
- Trust facts are a plain list with hairline rules, not badges or shields.
- Mobile first — most first visits are a phone scanning a QR code on a letter.
- The §41.0051 line lives in the footer of every page, bold, legible, not hidden in grey.

**Pages worth mocking up first:** Homepage · How it works · Pricing (it's one table — could fold into Homepage) · Address lookup / signup flow · FAQ. Skip the customer portal and commercial page for now.
